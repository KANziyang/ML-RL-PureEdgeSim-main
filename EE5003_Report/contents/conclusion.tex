\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{subsec:conclusion-summary}

This report investigated joint task offloading and PRB allocation in a three-tier LOCAL--EDGE--CLOUD architecture for 6G-oriented edge-cloud computing. The problem was formulated as a Dec-POMDP and addressed through a CTDE-based learning framework built on a customized PureEdgeSim environment with PRB-aware networking, device-level observations, and Java--Python co-simulation.

The first research question asked whether on-policy reinforcement learning can improve QoS and system efficiency over heuristic scheduling when destination selection and PRB reservation are controlled jointly. In this report, QoS is reflected primarily by task success rate and deadline-sensitive reliability, while average total time, energy consumption, and communication-resource usage are treated as efficiency or system-cost indicators. On this basis, the experimental evidence answers the first question positively. Across the standard 160-device setting, both MAPPO and PPO substantially outperform the heuristic baselines in task success rate and residual deadline failures while also remaining competitive or superior in average total time and energy consumption. In the offline comparison, they additionally achieve much lower mean bandwidth occupation than the heuristic schedulers, indicating that joint control over destination selection and PRB reservation improves not only service quality but also communication-resource efficiency.

The second research question asked which design elements are mainly responsible for this gain. Here the ablation results provide a clear answer: adaptive PRB control is the dominant contributor to final policy quality. Removing agent embeddings causes only a small degradation, whereas fixing PRB allocation leads to a large drop in success rate and a marked increase in energy consumption and delay failures. The strongest supported design conclusion of the report is therefore that joint offloading and adaptive wireless-resource control is the key principle behind the scheduler's performance in the studied setting, while explicit agent conditioning provides a smaller but stable reliability refinement.

The third research question asked whether MAPPO provides a meaningful advantage over a shared-policy PPO baseline. The evidence supports a qualified rather than absolute answer. PPO is marginally stronger on several three-seed mean metrics, so the present results do not justify claiming overall numerical superiority for MAPPO on aggregate averages. MAPPO's supported advantage is narrower: under the realized fixed-seed workload it yields fewer residual deadline failures, and the NoEmbedding ablation further indicates that explicit agent conditioning improves reliability under shared multi-terminal pressure. MAPPO should therefore be understood less as a universally stronger optimizer and more as a methodologically better-matched per-device formulation for the studied device-coupled system.

The device-count study places an explicit boundary on these conclusions. Under the current four-edge-plus-one-cloud infrastructure, MAPPO remains highly effective from 80 to 240 devices, but performance collapses at 320 devices because the workload exceeds the available capacity and delay failures surge sharply. The main conclusion is therefore not that learning removes infrastructure limits, but that it exploits the available compute-radio capacity effectively within the tested operating envelope.

Overall, the report supports on-policy reinforcement learning as a practical scheduler for the studied simulator-defined problem class, with adaptive PRB control as the dominant source of performance gain. MAPPO is best understood as a structurally well-matched per-device formulation with a narrower reliability-oriented advantage, rather than an across-the-board numerical win over PPO.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:conclusion-limitations}

Several limitations define the current boundary of this study. First, the evidence remains simulation-based, so a non-trivial gap still separates the modeled PRB-aware wireless environment from a real 5G or 6G deployment. Second, the current claims are tied to the tested workload range and to the specific four-edge-plus-one-cloud infrastructure used here. The 320-device result shows that policy optimization alone does not overcome capacity saturation, so the scalability conclusion should be read as a capacity-envelope result rather than as proof of robustness at arbitrary scale. Third, the present formulation studies full-task offloading only; it does not yet address partial offloading, where the scheduler would need to coordinate task partitioning together with destination selection and communication-resource allocation. Fourth, the evaluated architecture is limited to the LOCAL--EDGE--CLOUD setting and therefore does not yet cover a richer continuum in which neighboring devices or an explicit mist layer can also participate in execution.

Methodologically, the current MAPPO design should also be viewed as a strong but not exhaustive point in the design space. The critic input, reward coefficients, agent representation, and training-stability strategy were chosen carefully for this report, but a broader search over these factors would strengthen the claims further. Robustness validation also remains limited relative to the full ambition of the problem: although the preserved seeds and device-count study show stable behavior within the tested regime, broader verification across more seeds, traffic patterns, and topology perturbations is still needed. Finally, the finalized PPO artifacts do not preserve the same per-decision trajectory exports as MAPPO, so the aggregate PPO metrics are trustworthy but the behavioral comparison for the third research question is not yet fully symmetric at the action level.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{subsec:conclusion-future}

The current results suggest several direct directions for future work.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Map the capacity boundary more systematically by varying device density together with edge/cloud compute capacity and PRB budgets, so that the 320-device saturation point can be quantified and relieved through matched infrastructure scaling rather than algorithm tuning alone.
    \item Preserve symmetric per-decision behavioral artifacts for PPO and MAPPO and extend evaluation to more seeds, traffic mixes, and topology perturbations, enabling a stronger action-level comparison between shared-policy and multi-agent designs.
    \item Extend the current full-offloading formulation to partial offloading, so that the scheduler jointly decides how a task should be partitioned, where each part should execute, and how the associated communication resources should be allocated.
    \item Expand the architecture beyond LOCAL--EDGE--CLOUD by introducing an explicit mist layer or neighboring-device execution, allowing tasks to be offloaded to nearby devices and thereby moving toward a fuller computing-continuum formulation.
    \item Reduce the simulation-to-reality gap by coupling the current framework with more realistic traffic traces or a small-scale hardware testbed.
\end{itemize}

Overall, the current evidence supports on-policy reinforcement learning as an effective way to use the available compute and wireless resources in the studied hierarchical edge-cloud system. Within that capacity envelope, adaptive PRB control is the dominant design factor, and MAPPO should be viewed as a reliability-oriented, structurally aligned formulation rather than as an unconditional aggregate-metric winner over PPO.
